# Related works

| Paper title | Link | Paper summary | Why it is relevant |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Combinatorial Ski Rental Problem: Robust and Learning-Augmented Algorithms | [NeurIPS 2025](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ae3e173398d6e43fea63cbcc16fbaa98-Abstract-Conference.html) | This paper studies online decisions to rent or buy several items individually or in bundles without knowing the future horizon. It gives an optimal randomized algorithm for its setting and extends it with learned predictions. | Verifying repeatedly versus paying once to repair resembles renting versus buying under unknown future use. Its multiple-item setting may inform a later extension in which repairs to related memories share costs. |
| Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262) | The authors use causal interventions to locate computations involved in a language model's factual predictions. Their ROME method edits model weights to change individual factual associations. | It illustrates one way to update knowledge after a fact changes. Our proposed repair updates an external derived-memory component, so its editing mechanism and cost cannot be treated as a direct baseline without further work. |
| Mass-Editing Memory in a Transformer | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07229) | MEMIT updates many factual associations directly in a language model's weights. The authors demonstrate edits at a scale of thousands of associations in the models they evaluate. | Its treatment of multiple edits motivates asking whether grouped repairs can be cheaper than isolated ones. Our core experiment instead asks when to perform an external-memory repair at all. |
| All-Mem: Agentic Lifelong Memory via Dynamic Topology Evolution | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19595) | All-Mem combines bounded online retrieval with periodic offline changes to a structured memory bank. It uses split, merge, and update operations while retaining underlying evidence for later access. | It is a close memory-maintenance comparison for our query-triggered decisions. Its broader retrieval and topology mechanisms mean that a direct numerical comparison would need carefully matched tasks and costs. |
| EchoEdit: Consistent Multi-Hop Question Answering via Ripple Control in Knowledge Editing | [AAAI 2026](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/39013) | EchoEdit models knowledge potentially affected by an edit with a RippleGraph and generates rules to control propagation. It evaluates consistency on multi-hop question-answering tasks after edits. | It highlights how a changed fact can affect dependent answers. Our project can use explicit dependencies to identify stale components while studying the separate choice of whether to verify or repair them. |
| LongMemEval-V2: Evaluating Long-Term Agent Memory Toward Experienced Colleagues | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12493) | LongMemEval-V2 evaluates whether web agents can use past environment trajectories to answer questions about state, workflows, and pitfalls. It includes 451 curated questions and compares retrieval approaches with different accuracy and latency. | It offers examples of long-term agent memory demands and their latency tradeoffs. Its trajectory questions are not automatically correction-and-repair events, so using it here would require a justified mapping. |
| Improving Online Algorithms via ML Predictions | [NeurIPS 2018](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/hash/73a427badebe0e32caa2e1fc7530b7f3-Abstract.html) | The authors develop prediction-assisted online algorithms for ski rental and job scheduling. Their algorithms improve with good predictions while limiting the harm from poor ones. | This provides a framework for evaluating a policy that predicts future queries before choosing repair. It also motivates comparing such a policy with a simple threshold when predictions are inaccurate. |
| LongMemEval: Benchmarking Chat Assistants on Long-Term Interactive Memory | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813) | LongMemEval evaluates long-term memory in chat assistants across tasks involving past interactions, including knowledge updates and temporal reasoning. It tests whether systems can retrieve and use relevant information from long histories. | Its update questions are a possible realistic check on whether conclusions from synthetic events transfer. We would still need to establish how its examples specify corrections, future access, and repair costs before using it to compare policies. |
